## How to read this layer

Each dimension has a definition followed by five maturity levels and realistically adjacent transitions. Each transition ends with a verification statement: the observable that proves the destination level is reached rather than claimed. Maturity does not mean "more AI." AI contributes only where it improves continuity, shared intelligence, evidence quality, cross-functional consumption, or context-specific rendering, without creating separate departmental truths.

The three dimensions remain independently scoreable through Levels A–D. At Level E, they converge into one continuously maintained intelligence layer:

- **D1** contributes the market and opportunity lens.
- **D2** contributes the buyer and user lens.
- **D3** contributes the positioning, competitive, and strategic differentiation lens.

Function-specific outputs may differ, but they are renderings of the same underlying intelligence — not independently commissioned analyses.

The unit of these three dimensions is the state of the organization's intelligence — whether the answer to a question is already there, and current, when the question arrives — not the events that update it; a rendering is one audience's form of intelligence the layer already holds.

**Reading from a chair.** Each model that inherits these three dimensions reads them from its own function's chair. Through Level C the text describes one function's asset, and a model scores the asset its function operates, or, where its function keeps none of its own, the asset the organisation keeps, whichever function keeps it; where more than one function keeps one, the highest level that holds with evidence, as the scoring instrument's select rule reads it. Where more than one function maintains a version of the same asset, Product's user personas beside Marketing's buyer personas, each model scores its own; the dimension is below D by definition, and the distance between the models' scores is the measure of how far apart the versions are, not a scoring error. Level D requires one asset that no function duplicates, so from D upward every chair scores the same letter.

## D1. Market discovery & definition

*The capability to identify, size, and validate market opportunities — including TAM/SAM/SOM, market trends, and opportunity framing — through a continuous, AI-assisted discovery loop that supplies a shared intelligence foundation for requirements, prioritization, and portfolio investment decisions.*

### A — Nascent

Discovery happens periodically, such as during planning cycles, or when a question demands it, and each time it is manually synthesized from scratch. AI use is inconsistent and left to individual researcher preference, with no shared method.

**Transition from A to B**

Establish a consistent AI-assisted method for market-opportunity analysis and one kept corpus that its outputs land in. Define the minimum outputs required — including TAM/SAM/SOM, trend analysis, and opportunity framing — and produce them by the same method into the same corpus every time the work is done, whether a planning cycle or a question occasioned it.

**Verification:** The minimum output set — TAM/SAM/SOM, trend analysis, opportunity framing — exists in one kept corpus, produced by one written AI-assisted method, each output dated, and the corpus has been worked at least twice by that method, the later work revising the earlier, in place or as a new edition that carries the earlier forward and states what changed, rather than starting again.

### B — Modeled

Discovery has a consistent AI-assisted method and one kept corpus, but the corpus is as current as the last time it was worked — a planning cycle, a quarter, or the last question that sent someone back to it — so a question can find it stale and wait for the work. It remains one function's exercise, typically Product's: outputs are produced for that function's own use and are not routinely shared with the others that need them, whether Product, Marketing, Sales, or Strategy.

**Transition from B to C**

Move from a corpus worked in episodes to one kept current. Work the market signals, trend analysis, and opportunity model continuously, so that a material market shift is in the corpus before anyone asks about it and a question is answered from the corpus as it stands. The test is not how often the corpus is updated but whether a question has to wait for it.

**Verification:** A material market shift is in the opportunity model, dated, before anyone asks about it, and a question is answered from the model as it already stood, the entries that answer it dated before the question, with any question that had to wait for new work recorded as a gap.

### C — Continuous

Discovery keeps the corpus current rather than updating it in episodes. Market signals, trend analysis, and opportunity models are worked on an ongoing basis, so the answer to a question is already there when the question arrives. The intelligence remains one function's, typically Product's, although other functions can request access to current findings.

**Transition from C to D**

Open the discovery model as a shared asset. Product, Marketing, Sales Enablement, and Strategy should draw directly from the same underlying market and opportunity intelligence rather than request a summary from the function that maintains it, and no function should keep a version of its own. Define which outputs each function consumes and establish the access path.

**Verification:** A participant from a function other than the maintaining one retrieves current market and opportunity intelligence from the shared model directly, without a request to the maintaining function and without it producing a summary, and no function holds a separately maintained opportunity model.

### D — Integral

The continuous discovery model is a shared asset. Product, Marketing, Sales Enablement, and Strategy draw directly from the same underlying market and opportunity model rather than commissioning separate research or maintaining a version of their own. Each function still produces its own context-specific rendering manually.

**Transition from D to E**

Converge D1, D2, and D3 as one shared intelligence layer and automate function-specific rendering. A material market update should propagate into the relevant Product, Marketing, Sales, and Strategy outputs without a separate commissioning or human reformulation step.

**Verification:** A material market update propagates into the Product, Marketing, Sales, and Strategy renderings from the one layer, each in its audience's form, without a separate commissioning or human reformulation step — and the renderings agree with one another.

### E — Telemetric

D1, D2, and D3 operate as one converged intelligence layer that automatically generates function-specific renderings — including Product roadmap and requirements input, Marketing messaging, Sales battlecards, and Strategy narratives — from the same continuously updated model, each adapted to its audience without separate commissioning.

**Vantage:** From D1's vantage, market signals, trend data, and opportunity models are the input lens this layer continuously refreshes.

**Sustainment:** Verify that automatic renderings accurately reflect the shared intelligence and that no consuming function is silently receiving stale or miscontextualized output. Audit D1–D3 convergence periodically to confirm that the layer is coherent, not merely synchronized.

## D2. Buyer & user persona development

*The capability to research, model, and maintain live representations of target users and buyers as a shared intelligence asset across Product, Marketing, Sales Enablement, and Engineering.*

### A — Nascent

Buyer and user personas exist as static documents created at a point in time, such as a launch or major redesign, and are rarely revisited, whichever function keeps them. AI use, where present, is inconsistent and left to individual preference.

**Transition from A to B**

Establish a shared persona structure, clear ownership, and one kept set for both buyer and user personas, with a stated refresh rule — a cadence, a condition such as a launch or a decision that needs them current, or both. Adopt a consistent AI-assisted research and synthesis method rather than relying on departmental or individual practice.

**Verification:** Buyer and user personas share one structure, name an owner, carry the date of their last refresh and either the date of the next or the condition that occasions one, and at least one refresh has happened as the set said it would, by the method, revising the set in place or as a new edition that carries the earlier forward and states what changed.

### B — Modeled

Buyer and user personas are refreshed in episodes — on a defined cadence, or when a launch or a decision needs them current — using a consistent AI-assisted research and synthesis method and selected research, usage, or feedback data, and each refresh revises the one kept set. They remain one function's, typically Product's for user personas and Marketing's for buyer personas. Other functions receive periodic document renderings, or nothing, rather than consuming a shared, live persona-intelligence system directly.

**Transition from B to C**

Move from personas refreshed in episodes and distributed as documents to a live shared intelligence system. Connect persona attributes to ongoing research, usage, and feedback signals; establish common underlying data and AI intelligence; and extend direct access across Product, Marketing, Sales, and Engineering.

**Verification:** A new research, usage, or feedback signal changes a persona attribute in the shared system without a document being reissued, and a Marketing, Sales, or Engineering participant sees the change at the source.

### C — Continuous

Buyer and user personas are maintained in a shared system with common update data and AI intelligence. Usage, research, and feedback signals refresh the representations on an ongoing basis. Access extends across Product, Marketing, Sales, and Engineering, although the intelligence remains one function's to maintain, typically Product's, and consuming functions still interpret it for their own uses.

**Transition from C to D**

Make personas a directly consumed shared asset rather than a document distributed by the maintaining function. Product, Marketing, and Sales Enablement should draw from the same persona intelligence for their work, with no separately maintained set in any of them, while Engineering receives relevant user context through the requirements management system.

**Verification:** Product, Marketing, and Sales Enablement work from the shared persona intelligence with no separately maintained persona in any of them, and Engineering reaches relevant user context from inside the requirements management system rather than from a distributed document.

### D — Integral

Personas are dynamic, continuously maintained shared assets fed by usage, research, and feedback data. Product, Marketing, and Sales Enablement consume them directly rather than commissioning or maintaining separate personas; each buyer and user persona is kept once, in the shared set. Engineering has connected access to relevant user context through the requirements management system. Each function still produces its own context-specific rendering manually.

**Transition from D to E**

Converge D1, D2, and D3 as one shared intelligence layer and automate function-specific persona rendering. A meaningful update to buyer or user intelligence should propagate into the relevant Product, Marketing, Sales, Engineering, and Strategy outputs without manual reformulation.

**Verification:** A meaningful change to buyer or user intelligence propagates into the Product, Marketing, Sales, Engineering, and Strategy renderings without manual reformulation, and each rendering remains faithful to the underlying persona.

### E — Telemetric

D1, D2, and D3 operate as one converged intelligence layer that automatically generates function-specific renderings — including Product roadmap and requirements input, Marketing messaging, Sales battlecards, and Strategy narratives — from the same continuously updated model, each adapted to its audience without separate commissioning.

**Vantage:** From D2's vantage, live representations of target users and buyers are the input lens this layer continuously refreshes, extending to Engineering through the requirements management system.

**Sustainment:** Verify that automatically generated persona renderings remain faithful to the underlying intelligence and have not drifted because of stale data, automation bias, or function-specific reinterpretation. Audit D1–D3 convergence periodically.

## D3. Positioning & competitive intelligence

*The capability to define product differentiation and maintain that positioning against a continuously monitored competitive landscape, actively informing roadmap, prioritization, pricing, go-to-market, and portfolio decisions while preserving a current strategic thesis about what the organization intends to do better than competitors.*

### A — Nascent

Positioning was defined at a point in time, such as product launch, and competitive awareness is ad hoc or anecdotal. AI use, where present, is inconsistent and left to individual preference, with no shared method.

**Transition from A to B**

Establish a consistent AI-assisted competitive-monitoring method and one kept record of the landscape. Require a minimum output set — including the competitor landscape, messaging differentiation, and positioning thesis — produced into that record by the same method every time the work is done, rather than analysis of only the competitor or threat at hand.

**Verification:** The minimum output set — competitor landscape, messaging differentiation, positioning thesis — exists in one kept record, produced by one written AI-assisted method, each entry dated, and the record has been worked at least twice by that method, the later work revising the earlier, in place or as a new edition that carries the earlier forward and states what changed, rather than starting again, and the latest work was not only a response to a visible competitive threat.

### B — Modeled

Competitive monitoring has a consistent AI-assisted method and one kept record, but the record is as current as the last time it was worked — a scheduled scan, or the last question that sent someone back to it — so a question can find it stale and wait for the work. It remains one function's exercise, typically Product's, Strategy's, or Marketing's: findings inform that function's own roadmap and positioning discussions but are not routinely packaged for the other functions that need them.

**Transition from B to C**

Move from a record worked in episodes to a landscape kept current. Work the competitor, market, pricing, and messaging signals continuously, so that a competitor's change is in the record before anyone asks about it and a question is answered from the record as it stands. Maintain the positioning and strategic differentiation thesis as living intelligence rather than a launch artifact.

**Verification:** A competitor's pricing, product, or messaging change is in the competitive record, dated, before anyone asks about it or circulates it by hand; a question about the landscape is answered from the record as it already stood, with any question that had to wait for new work recorded as a gap; and the positioning thesis carries a revision dated after launch.

### C — Continuous

Competitive monitoring keeps the landscape current rather than scanning it in episodes. Market, pricing, product, and messaging shifts are worked into the record on an ongoing basis, so the answer to a question is already there when it arrives. Positioning and the strategic differentiation thesis remain primarily owned by Product or Strategy, although other functions can request access to current intelligence.

**Transition from C to D**

Open the competitive model as a shared asset. Product, Marketing, and Sales Enablement should draw directly from the same underlying intelligence for roadmap input, messaging, and battlecards, with no separately maintained competitive model in any of them. Maintain the strategic differentiation thesis — what the organization intends to do better than competitors — as an explicit, versioned, and auditable artifact.

**Verification:** Product, Marketing, and Sales Enablement build roadmap input, messaging, and battlecards from the shared competitive model rather than from commissioned or separately maintained research, and the strategic differentiation thesis can be read at a named version with its change history.

### D — Integral

The competitive record, kept current, is a shared asset. Product, Marketing, and Sales Enablement draw directly from the same underlying competitive model rather than commissioning separate research or maintaining a record of their own. The organization's positioning and strategic differentiation thesis are explicitly maintained and versioned as living artifacts. Each function still produces its own context-specific rendering manually.

**Transition from D to E**

Converge D1, D2, and D3 as one shared intelligence layer and automate function-specific rendering. Competitive, positioning, and differentiation changes should propagate into Product, Marketing, Sales, Pricing, and Strategy outputs without a separate commissioning or human reformulation step.

**Verification:** A competitive, positioning, or differentiation change propagates into Product, Marketing, Sales, Pricing, and Strategy renderings without a separate commissioning or reformulation step, and the thesis the renderings express is the one at the current version.

### E — Telemetric

D1, D2, and D3 operate as one converged intelligence layer that automatically generates function-specific renderings — including Product roadmap and requirements input, Marketing messaging, Sales battlecards, and Strategy narratives — from the same continuously updated model, each adapted to its audience without separate commissioning.

**Vantage:** From D3's vantage, positioning, the live competitive landscape, and the organization's strategic differentiation thesis are the input lens this layer continuously refreshes.

**Sustainment:** Verify that automatic competitive and positioning renderings accurately reflect the underlying intelligence and that the strategic differentiation thesis has not silently drifted through automation or local reinterpretation. Audit D1–D3 convergence periodically.

## Shared Level E convergence rule

Level E is not three synchronized repositories or three departmental systems exchanging updates. It is one governed intelligence layer with three distinct analytical lenses.

A source change should update the shared model once and produce the appropriate downstream renderings automatically. The system may adapt vocabulary, emphasis, and format for each audience, but it must preserve the same underlying market, persona, competitive, and strategic meaning.

The convergence test is:

> **Can one meaningful change in the underlying intelligence propagate into every affected downstream use without separate research, manual reconciliation, or silent semantic divergence?**

## Lifecycle interfaces

The following interfaces describe how each lifecycle consumes the shared layer. They do not alter the inherited D1–D3 maturity definitions.

**AI-Native SDLC** consumes the intelligence layer primarily through:

- **D4 Requirements management:** market, persona, positioning, and competitive intelligence become specification context and traceable requirements input.
- **D5 Prototyping:** prototypes validate interpretations of market opportunity, persona needs, and positioning claims.
- **D12 Instrumentation & observability:** production and organizational evidence returns to refresh the shared intelligence layer.
- **D13 Feedback loop velocity:** measures how quickly a market, user, competitive, or operational signal becomes a shipped response.

**AI-Native PDLC** consumes the intelligence layer primarily through:

- **D4 Requirements management:** intelligence becomes precise product definition and traceable specification.
- **D5 Prioritization & tradeoffs:** opportunities, personas, positioning, and differentiation become explicit value-model inputs.
- **D8 Portfolio & investment management:** shared intelligence informs investment thresholds, allocation, sequencing, and portfolio balance.
- **D9 GTM cadence & market activation:** Marketing and Sales consume function-specific renderings from the same intelligence layer.
- **D11 Analytics & outcome measurement:** realized outcomes test the market, persona, positioning, and differentiation assumptions that informed the decision.
- **D12 Feedback loop velocity:** measures how quickly evidence completes the loop back into product and portfolio decisions.
